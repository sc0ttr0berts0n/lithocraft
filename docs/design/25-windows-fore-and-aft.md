# The windows fore and aft (28 November 2026)

A grill of its own on the developer's idea from round 1 of `24-after-first-full-dive.md`, the play test of 28 November 2026.

- **Each end cap of the Habitat has a wide ring of window round the Bore**, so with down along the axis one is always the floor: diving, the crew stands on glass over the tokamak; climbing under thrust, over the exhaust.
- **The front window shows the plasma as a real one looks**: its hot core is invisible, its cooler edge glows pink-violet in hydrogen's red and blue lines, with bright spots where it meets the wall; the Bore's glass passes through its hole.
  The edge's shimmer is driven by the Wobble the simulation already has, where practical (the developer).
- **The funnel**: the rim cuts a ring of white-hot melt pumped outward round a dark disc of uncut rock, and the Column's skin, grazed by the rim, glows orange and cools to plain rock over its first metres toward the Habitat; each colour is a real temperature.
- **The plasma is a live gauge**: a steady glow with the Residual in the Band; outside it, bright bursts flick off the edge onto the wall (edge-localised modes); Head wear leaves hot spots on the wall tiles; a lost plasma flashes and goes dark (a disruption); a relight is a pink flash filling the chamber as the gas breaks down.
- **The Tail melts the Column as it leaves** and presses the melt into the tunnel wall's glass, so no pillar of rock stands down the tunnel behind: the aft window shows the Column's end slumping into glowing melt smeared onto the wall.
  It is laid like a glue gun's bead: a nozzle turning round the Bore winds the melt onto the wall in a glowing spiral, each turn joining the last as the vessel advances, orange fading to dark glass behind (the developer's image; real basis: a weld bead, a 3D printer's nozzle).
  It costs little (the Column is under 2% of the cross-section) and keeps the tunnel open for a rescue. "That's cooler than I had considered … the Column oozing out like a glue gun honestly sounds so cool."
- **In a Glide the windows darken like a welding filter**, by themselves, rather than shut: forward, the funnel becomes melt streaks rushing in; aft, the jet leaves the magnetic nozzle with shock diamonds, as a supersonic exhaust shows.
- **The windows light the room**: the plasma's pink-violet comes up through the floor and colours the Habitat as the Glide's melt does, so the room tells the state at a glance, violet working, orange gliding.
  At Quiet Ship the windows stay open and the interior falls to a very dim blue, its lamps a minimum, plus whatever light the rock itself gives, the cooling melt and the Column's skin.
  Glass makes no noise, so a clean pulse never needed them shut; the shutters close only in emergencies.
- **Visible and cool rather than the realest real**: brightness and size are exaggerated, colours and relationships kept, so a worse plasma is always brighter and burstier (ADR 0001).
- **Built in Godot, judged in pictures**: a screenshot recipe and a debug command forcing each plasma state, read by the looker (`docs/agents/waves.md`); no browser mock, since it is light, glass and particles.
- **The Cockpit sees the funnel straight on**: it respects the Head's orientation, the Arm respects gravity, so from the Arm the funnel is seen at an angle across the floor.
