# The Habitat and the crew

- **Walkable**, not a cockpit.
  Small: one ring, six Stations.
- **"Down" is whatever the real acceleration is.**
  Diving, it points at the Head.
  Under thrust, at the Tail.
  Spinning in space, outward at the hull.
  Level, at the bottom of the outer wall.
  Stations hang on gimbals.
  The developer's rotating console becomes the rule for the whole interior.
- **Six Stations:** Reactor, Drive, Thermal, Navigation, Bore Station, Fabricator.
  Each maps to one design decision; none exists only to fill a seat.
- **Crew of one to four.**
  The developer felt multiplayer from the start makes sense, and asked how to keep a full crew busy while still playing well alone.
  The answer: each Station has an autopilot that is safe but mediocre, and a human there gets more out of it.
  Solo, you move between Stations and choose which deserves you.
  A full crew is busy because every Station rewards attention.
- **Build order.**
  The simulation is kept separate from the view from day one so networking can be added without a rewrite, but solo play is made fun first.
- **Conflicts come from three shared budgets.**
  Power (the Reactor supplies; everything else draws), heat (everything makes it; Thermal decides who suffers) and the Column (Thermal wants to cook it; the Bore Station wants it pristine).
  No conflicts are scripted.
  The developer is interested in conflicts between Stations being something to manage.
- **Calling for attention.**
  Signals inside the vessel only.
  Each Station has its own tone and light, and because the Habitat is a ring you can hear the direction.
  Urgency is in pitch and tempo.
  No floating markers.
  The developer stressed that a Station needing a human must be telegraphed well.
- **Systems management, not stick flying.**
  You set heading, power, grip and heat routing and the vessel responds slowly.
  An ascent is a procedure, not joystick flying.
  One control style covers every Medium.
- **Navigation plots, the Drive steers (25 November 2026).**
  Navigation sets the Course (a target heading and pitch) on the point cloud, where the Medium ahead is seen.
  The Drive steers toward it: how hard to turn, and so how much power and heat to spend, is the Drive's call, and it can override.
  An unattended Drive follows the Course at a safe, slow turn rate.
  Chosen over heading on the Drive alone (Navigation was passive) and heading on Navigation alone (steering's power and heat cost belongs with the Drive).
  Real precedent: a ship's navigator plots the course and the helmsman steers it.
  *Superseded in part by "Navigation plans, the Drive deviates" (After the second checkpoint): the Drive goes off the Course only by nudging pitch and heading.*
