# The plasma, waves and fabrication

## The plasma and the Shift

- The plasma is the one thing that cannot be ignored.
  Harder rock loads the Head; a sudden void unloads it.
  Either can destabilise the plasma.
- Crossing a Boundary requires a Shift: retuning the plasma for the new load within a window.
  The developer's image: "like poorly shifting gears."
- A clean Shift is smooth.
  A bad one grinds: a heat spike, a lurch, wear on the Head.
  Several bad Shifts in a row, or one at the wrong depth, loses the plasma.
- The Reactor autopilot holds steady conditions but handles sudden changes badly, so Boundaries are where a human is needed.
  This gives the game its rhythm: calm stretches, tense Boundaries.

## Waves as the common language

- The developer wants to do something with Fourier ideas: lining up sine waves to make other shapes, for molecular synthesis.
- **Real science behind it.**
  Each kind of ion circles a magnetic field at its own frequency, so one element can be picked out by tuning to it.
  Chemists already steer reactions with light pulses built by adding chosen frequencies.
  Sorting a plasma into its elements with magnetic fields is a real proposed technique.
- **Fabrication as wave-building.**
  Each thing you can make has a target waveform.
  You pick Feedstocks by tuning to their frequencies, then stack and adjust sine waves until the sum matches the target.
  A closer match gives a better part; a sloppy match gives a part that works but wears out sooner.
  Skill produces quality, not pass or fail, so a rushed repair in a crisis is possible but costs later.
- **Every Station speaks in waves.**
  The Reactor's plasma wobbles in modes you cancel with opposing waves.
  Navigation's scan is pulses and echoes.
  The Fabricator stacks sines.
  The Bore Station reads composition as spectral lines.
  One skill learned once applies everywhere, and the displays share a look.

## Feedstock and fabrication

- The developer's idea: grabbing atoms and molecules like printer ink to synthesise things.
- The Head is also the refinery.
  Some of the melt is ionised and magnetically sorted by element into Feedstock: iron, silicon, carbon, water and so on.
- What a part costs is a recipe of elements, so the rock you are in decides what you can repair right now.
  A basalt dive is rich in iron and poor in carbon.
- **Print versus buy.**
  Print consumables and replacement parts from Feedstock.
  Buy designs for new parts with survey money.
  Hull grades need both a bought design and deep materials you fetched yourself.
- **Repairs by hand.**
  Parts are physical objects in the Habitat that you pull, inspect and replace, as in Uncle Chop's Rocket Shop.
