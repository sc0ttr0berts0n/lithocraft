# The vessel and its Media

## The vessel

- **Shape.**
  A straw in three sections: Head, Habitat, Tail.
  The Bore runs through all three.
- **The Head is the tokamak.**
  A tokamak is already a ring with a hole through the middle, so the reactor's shape is the vessel's shape and the Bore passes through the hole.
  The developer's idea was that the tokamak is the heated cutting end and that its magnetism keeps high heat off the inside.
- **Honest limits on the magnetism.**
  Magnets do nothing to cold rock.
  They act only on things that conduct: molten rock, ionised gas and the plasma itself.
  Fusion heat mostly arrives as radiation absorbed in the structure and must be carried to the cutting rim.
  Magnetic fields therefore have three jobs: confine the plasma, pump molten rock outward, and form the exhaust nozzle.
- **The Bore.**
  About 1.5 m across ("we can change as needed").
  The Column sliding through it is the game's signature image and the crew's main instrument.
  "That's the gimmick."
- **Coring is not an energy saving.**
  With a 1.5 m Column inside a ring perhaps 12 m across, the Column is under 2% of the cross-section.
  Nearly all the rock is still removed.
  Coring is kept for what it shows, not what it saves.
- **Single Head, no reverse.**
  To come back up the vessel steers a wide arc by heating one side of the rim harder.
  A turning circle of hundreds of metres makes route planning a real decision.
  A second Head at the Tail was considered and not chosen.

## One principle for every Medium

The Lithocraft is a reactor wrapped around a duct, and whatever is in the duct gets heated.

- **Rock.**
  Melted, and the melt pressed into the tunnel wall as a glass lining.
  Real precedent: the Los Alamos "subterrene" of the 1970s.
- **Air.**
  Heated and expelled as a nuclear ramjet.
  Real precedent: Project Pluto.
- **Vacuum.**
  Stored propellant fed through and heated, as a nuclear thermal rocket.
- **Gas giant.**
  The same ramjet, breathing hydrogen.

This answers how a vessel optimised for rock reaches space, and it makes the ring shape necessary instead of decorative.
Three separate drive systems were the alternative and were rejected.

**Ascent to orbit, in three steps.**
Leave the ground on stored propellant heated by the Head; switch to breathing air through the Bore once moving fast enough; return to stored propellant above the atmosphere.
Water is the propellant, taken from ice or wet rock, so a dive also has to bring back enough water to leave.

## Moving through rock

- **Worm drive.**
  Gripper pads clamp the tunnel wall and move in travelling waves down the hull, so the vessel crawls like the Dune worm.
  Alternatives considered: weight alone (only works going down) and thrust from squirting melt backward.
- **Materials decide efficiency.**
  Grip depends on rock strength.
  Hard rock grips well but cuts slowly; loose sand cuts easily but gives nothing to push against.
  The developer liked materials driving how efficient the system is, "or if it's needed at all" — in some Mediums the grippers may be unnecessary.
- **Where the melt goes.**
  Porous rock absorbs it as lining and leaves an open tunnel.
  Dense rock has no pore space, so the melt is pumped aft and fills the tunnel.
  Shallow, you leave a retreat path.
  Deep, you seal yourself in, and the player watches that transition approach.
  At depth the rock also creeps shut on its own, like a submarine below crush depth.
- **A Medium is seven properties.**
  Hardness (cost to cut), strength (grip available), porosity (open tunnel or sealed), temperature, pressure, composition (Feedstock yield and survey value), and how well it carries sound (sensing and comms range).
  Every system reads the same seven.
  Air and vacuum are the same list with most values near zero.
  The developer's reaction: "this sounds extensible."
- **Honest speeds with time acceleration.**
  Around a metre per second in hard rock, much faster in sand, air and space, with Kerbal-style time warp that drops out when something needs attention.
  Power sets speed; heat limits power.
